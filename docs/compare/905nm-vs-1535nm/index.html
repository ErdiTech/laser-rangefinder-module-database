905 nm vs 1535 nm laser rangefinder modules (2026)

Updated: Nov 2026

Short answer: 905 nm modules (silicon avalanche photodiode receivers, pulsed laser diodes) are the low-cost, low-weight choice and in this dataset reach up to 3 km on large reflective targets; 1535 nm modules (InGaAs receivers, usually erbium-glass lasers) are eye-safe at much higher pulse energy, which is why they dominate multi-kilometre ranging, at higher price and weight. The figures below are computed from 152 modules in the open dataset (data date 2026-11-17).

How to choose

  • Up to a few hundred metres, small drones and robots: 905 nm (or 850/940 nm ToF) modules are lighter and cheaper; check update rate and interface.
  • Around 1-3 km: both exist. In this dataset the 905 nm modules rated 1-3 km weigh 5-34 g (33 with published weight) and the 1535 nm modules rated up to 3 km weigh 29-190 g (4 with published weight). Makers quote these ranges under different target sizes and reflectances, so compare the range condition column, not just the number.
  • Beyond 2-3 km, or where Class 1 eye safety at high energy is required: 1535 nm.
  • Both wavelengths are invisible; for aiming, some modules add a visible pointer or are boresighted to a camera.
